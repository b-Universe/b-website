Dev Blog #5:
Designing the User Interface

Client Preferences & Theming

I've always went through and changed my mind frequently when designing the UI you'll see here; the Settings Window lets you customize and change how the game looks and controls without digging through buried sub-menus or restarting your game.

Unlike a lot of MMO's you'll see, B doesn't bake straight images; you can pick a color or use one of the presets and the UI on this page and it'll update just like it does in-game without needing a refresh. This article is interactive!

The General tab handles quick toggles like auto-targeting, private messages, and locked power trays; the Interface is for customizing your UI directly, and Keybinds tab lets you click any action and press a new key or combo to rebind it. Customization to the Moon!

The Chat Window

The Chat Window normally sits in the corner of your screen and handles everything from local private messages to system notices. You can resize it, dock it, or move it around wherever it fits best to you!

Right on the header, you'll find quick shortcuts to open your Party, Friends, Alliance, and Email windows. The + and - buttons let you pop out extra chat boxes or collapse them when you don't need them.

Tabs keep chatter organized between General, Alliance, Social, and Combat, and you can create your own tabs with that + button. I also wired up unique features like a Discord relay chat so you can talk to folks on Discord right from in-game chat with a little Discord badge next to their names and a Reply feature! The chat box is interactive as well, which helps highlight replies someone's replying to you particularly. Each of the tabs can be customized to add or remove different chat channels, combat statistics, and system messages.

The Target & Party Windows

When a fight gets chaotic, you can't be squinting to figure out who's low on health. The Target Window and Party Window show you what's happening to you, your teammates, and your target at a glance.

The Target Window shows who you're currently fighting, along with their health, endurance, and active status effects like stuns or armor buffs. The health bar shifts from green down to yellow and red as their health drops, so you'll know when they're close to going down.

Right below that, your party list tracks everyone on your team with their health, endurance, and battery bars. You can spot the team leader by the gold star (★), see member archetypes like my character's slot showing my Controller, and quickly invite or leave with the buttons up top.

I'm thinking for now, the colors I've picked for the archetypes will likely need some color theory and adjustments, but for now, my primary focus is adjusting those indicator bars with options to adjust the interface with more clear health indicators; the idea is that some players may take interest in what's happening functionally to include each player's endurance and battery levels, and some players may take up the defensive method and might want to focus more on their team's health.

The Pet Command Window

Controlling your pets and henchmen shouldn't feel like a chore while you're in the middle of a fight; if you're playing an archetype prioritzing summoning entities to serve you like a drone class or sending commands to your henchmen in a more advanced attack, the Pet Command Window gives you a dashboard to manage your companions without it taking over your whole screen.

Each companion card shows their health, endurance, and battery so you know who needs attention. You'll notice while they reflect your current level, their strength corresponds to yours within your powersets (Like the [+2], or [±0] indicators) along with their tier badge.

The top buttons let you quickly set their stance to Passive, Defensive, or Aggressive, or you can instruct them to Attack, Follow, or Stay. Small status tags let you know what your pets are doing without cluttering up your screen.

The Email Window keeps you in the loop even when you're offline. If you've got refineries running or items listed on the market, your in-game profits, items, and trade receipts sent to you show up right in your inbox so you won't miss anything.

Autocratic vs Democratic Governance

Alliances can pick how decisions get made; your founding leader can choose to operate under an Autocracy where solely they decide everything for the group, your highest ranking officers can decide to run a Council Oligarchy where they vote on decisions together, you can collectively operate under a Democratic Consensus where everyone has a say when voting together.

These governances can require votes for big moves like kicking members or rank changes, and inactive members won't stall votes if they haven't logged in.

Multi-Tier Fine-Grained Permissions Matrix

Instead of locked-in roles, you get up to six custom ranks with individual permission toggles. You can turn on or off things like editing the MotD, spending the guild cash, or inviting members per rank; Even your ranks can be named whatever you and your alliance want!

It was important to me to include diversity here, so whether you looked at your alliance as a Guild, an Organization, or a union of like-minded supers, your alliance is unique to others.

Treasury Logistics & Active Coalition Perks

Members can pool money in the alliance bank to unlock perks for everyone. Perks like Shared Study Network for a +4% XP boost or a Shield Grid Resonance fir a +3% shield recharge apply to the whole guild, so everyone benefits when players chip in.

Alliances can also link together to form Coalitions - networks built when different groups connect with each other; when your alliance establishes a connection, it activates Active Coalition perks. These share even more bonuses across the coalition, so you don't miss out on even larger rewards while supporting other groups.

Anti-Stagnation Leader Autodemotion

If a guild leader goes missing, the guild won't get stuck forever. You can set an inactivity timer (between 14 and 90 days), and if the leader doesn't log in, leadership automatically passes down to the next active officer.

This keeps the alliance fully operational and ensures nobody loses access to bank funds or perks just because one person takes a break. If there aren't any active officers, the system will keep checking down the ranks until it finds an active member to take charge.

The Navigator & Zone Map

The Navigator Window with it's handy Map Window and objective related windows all sit at the top of your screen so you won't get lost or have to stop what you're doing just to check where to go or what to do next.

The Navigator shows a compass tape with tick marks, cardinal directions, and a red true-North indicator; your exact heading (like 45° NE) shows right beside it, and the buttons below give you quick access to your Map, Contacts, Missions, Clues, and Badges.

Underneath the compass by default mounts the Map Window, showing streets, neighborhoods, transit lines, and landmarks; it's all drawn as vectors so it stays sharp at any zoom level. Using the Drop Waypoint button drops a waypoint you can name, color-code, and customize an icon which shows up on your compass tape above. You can toggle each individual map layer on or off, and the scale bar adjusts as you zoom!

Contacts, Missions, & Clues

Missions in B aren't just generic exclamation marks on a map; you'll meet with contacts in person or call them up over the radio or a secured line. They'll hand you cases, police scanner calls, and objectives, and as you clear out hideouts you'll find clues, datapads, and souvenirs that piece together what's going on and unlock higher-level missions. Some detectives keep a close eye out for heists and bank robberies they may require assistance with!

Instead of burying these three parts across separate menus, I put them side by side. On the left you've got your contacts and radio comms; in the middle you've got your active missions, difficulty sliders, and police alerts; and on the right you've got your clues and audio recordings. You can check all of them at once without closing your windows if you utilize the docking and undocking control on the windows (The filled ○ toggle button on the header).

Badges & Accolades

If you were into the Boy or Girl Scouts, you know badges were half the fun. You weren't just going for a quick achievement; you were hiking miles of rugged trails, mastering wilderness survival, and coordinating tough community projects with your troop just to show off a prestigious rank like an Eagle Scout or a Golden Award on your sash.

The Badge Window brings that collector feel back. Badges are split across five main categories: Accolades, Exploration, Defeat, Accomplishments, and Milestones), with Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Platinum tiers. They aren't just for looks either; certain badges give permanent stat boosts like extra health, faster energy recharge rates, or whole new abilities and skills. Clicking any title you've unlocked wears it right away.
