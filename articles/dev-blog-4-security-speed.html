Dev Blog #4:
Security, Speed, and Object Culling

Shifting to Server Authority

When we initially prototyped the game, we took the easy route: the client told the server what it was doing. If you clicked an enemy, your browser would send a message to the backend saying, "I hit this monster for 50 damage." While this made development fast, it left the door wide open for script tampering and memory editing. Any player could open their developer console, change a variable, and instantly deal absurd damage.

We've officially ripped that out and replaced it with a strict Server-Authoritative architecture. The Node.js backend is now the absolute authority of the game, keeping every single calculation honest.

Anti-Cheat & State Validation

Your client no longer dictates damage, movement speed, or ability cooldowns. Instead, your client sends an "intent" to the server (e.g., "I'm trying to cast Fireball at these coordinates").

The server then verifies everything in real time: Do you have the energy? Is the cooldown finished? Is the target within line of sight? Are you actually standing where you say you are? If the math checks out, the server executes the action and broadcasts the results to all nearby players. This completely syncs multiplayer projectiles and permanently patches out speed hacks and range exploits.

Engine Rendering & Face Culling

Transitioning to WebGL using Three.js was a massive leap forward for us, but rendering thousands of voxel blocks comes with a heavy cost to the GPU. Initially, we were rendering every single face of every single block. If we generated a hill made of 10,000 dirt blocks, the engine was drawing 60,000 faces - even the ones buried deep underground that no player could ever see!

To solve this, we wrote a custom adjacency culling algorithm. Before the server sends a map chunk to the client, it checks the neighboring coordinates (x±1, y±1, z±1) of every single block. If a neighbor exists and is an opaque material, the engine skips pushing those hidden vertices to the GPU, giving us massive FPS gains!

Client-Side Prediction & Smoothing

Because of the new server authority, a new problem arose: latency. If your network ping is 100ms, waiting for the server to acknowledge your movement before updating your screen makes controls feel sluggish. To fix this, we implemented client-side prediction.

Now, when you press a movement key, your local client instantly begins simulating physics and interpolates your character locally. The server is still doing the real math in the background, but your client predicts where you should be between network ticks. If the server disagrees, it gently reconciles your position, keeping movement silky smooth.

Raycasting Math & Z-Up Coordinates

Taking a 2D mouse click on a flat monitor and projecting an invisible 3D laser into the world requires complex unprojection math.

Because our engine uses a custom Z-up coordinate system (where blocks are exactly 32 units large), standard tutorials didn't work for us. We wrote intersection logic specifically mapped against our scaled InstancedMesh matrices, ensuring that clicking the ground to teleport, interact, or build always finds the exact, pixel-perfect voxel in 3D space.

UI Architecture & Independent Zoom

Managing a complex UI in a browser-based game can get messy quickly. We've built an entirely independent HTML/CSS UI overlay that scales cleanly regardless of monitor resolution or aspect ratio.

We also introduced independent camera zooming: you can zoom your minimap way out to survey an entire region, while keeping your main gameplay camera zoomed in tightly for intense combat. An added Picture-in-Picture mode ensures you never lose sight of your character when examining full-screen maps.

Spherical Billboarding Math

Standard 2D sprites look unnatural when a 3D camera tilts over them; they tend to look like flat paper stuck to the floor. To solve this, we applied spherical billboarding math to our character meshes.

This logic dynamically calculates the pitch (rotation.x) of the sprite plane to match the camera's height. We programmed a 10-degree safety clamp so the tilt stops right before the camera's absolute limit. This guarantees the sprite always faces the player nicely while staying planted on the voxel ground, preserving our unique 2.5D aesthetic!
